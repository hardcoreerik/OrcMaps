## FeatureTile expansion (MEASURED, approximate)

Busy z14 tile `14/2593/5953`:

- 2,496 features, 5,814 paths, 23,776 vertices
- 6,049 property keys, ~113 KB of owned strings
- approximate FeatureTile owned bytes: ~1.2 MiB
- vs 142 KB decompressed MVT (~8×)

The preview tool streams one source tile: drop compressed bytes after
decompress, drop raw after MVT decode, drop `MvtTile` after translate,
render, then `FeatureTile::features.clear()`. Peak overlap during one
busy tile is approximately FeatureTile (~1.2 MiB) plus a shrinking
`MvtTile` during translate, plus render scratch. Do not keep a
viewport-wide FeatureTile cache.

The current `MvtTile` + `FeatureTile` deep copy is acceptable if the
engine keeps **one source tile** in RAM at a time (ESP32-P4 + PSRAM).
Holding a full 20-tile viewport of FeatureTiles would be ~20 MiB and
is not the intended embedded path. Layer-string duplication is real
(`layer` copied onto every feature) but not the first blocker.

## Render CPU path: double precision removed (0.2.3, host-verified)

Evidence class: **host render + shipped-binary disassembly. NOT device-measured.**

The `render_ms` figures above include per-vertex projection and per-segment
clipping. Both ran in IEEE double, which every ESP32 family emulates in
software (their FPUs are single-precision). Disassembly of the CYD firmware
shows `orcmap::ProjectLocal` making ~18 soft-float ROM calls per projected
vertex, and `orcmap::ClipLineToPixelRect` making ~50 per segment including
four `__divdf3`.

0.2.3 replaces both with integer arithmetic: `ProjectLocal` is Q16.16
(64-bit multiply + shift per axis) and the clipper keeps exact rationals.
Rendered output is byte-identical — host render (synthetic 1280x720) and
`pack-inspect preview` on Springfield z14/z15, world z2 and Oregon z7 — so
this is a speed and code-size change only, not a rendering change.

The effect on the device numbers above is **unmeasured**. Re-run the CYD
report on the same three packs before quoting a multiplier; `render_ms` is
the stage this change targets, `decode_ms` is not.

## Where a CYD frame's time actually goes (0.3.0, device-measured)

A 480x320 RGB565 frame is 307,200 bytes and `Panel_ST7796` only speaks
`rgb565_2Byte` or `rgb888_3Byte`, so bytes per frame is fixed by the hardware.
The fastest possible update is therefore one `fillScreen` at the highest SPI
clock: **30.98 ms at 80 MHz, or 32.3 fps**. Measured on the board, and the
panel read back exactly what was written at that clock.

The interesting part is that the CPU was never what limited it. A whole
202-feature frame costs ~2-3 ms of projection and clipping, while drawing it
straight to the panel cost 285.65 ms -- because 1 px road strokes went through
LovyanGFX `drawLine`, which issues a windowed SPI write per pixel: ~170 us per
12-pixel segment, 94% of the frame. Rasterising into RAM and pushing one band
at a time instead takes the same frame to **50.88 ms (19.7 fps)**, 5.6x faster.

30 fps is not reachable on this panel: pushing an *empty* frame as four bands
already costs 33.16 ms (30.2 fps), leaving nothing for drawing. Realistic
target is ~25 fps.

Full measurements, the per-step breakdown and the remaining work are in
`docs/evidence/2026-10-18-cyd-frame-rate.md`.
